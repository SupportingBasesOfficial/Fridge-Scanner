# FridgeScanner — Domain Invariants

## Status

DB-00 normative invariants. Later database, API, backend, frontend, jobs and integrations must preserve these rules.

## Identity and authorization

1. Household-scoped authority is granted through HouseholdMembership, not through a single global household role on User.
2. Every household-scoped operation must re-establish that the actor is authorized for the target Household.
3. A client-provided household identifier is context, never proof of authorization.
4. An entity owned by Household A must never be attached, moved or mutated through an entity owned by Household B unless a future explicit cross-household workflow defines both authorization and transfer semantics. Provider identity, integration credentials or external account ownership do not weaken this rule.

## Storage topology

5. Every Compartment belongs to exactly one StorageLocation; every StorageLocation belongs to exactly one Household.
6. Every stored StockItem has exactly one placement anchor: either one Compartment or one StorageLocation directly. If anchored to a Compartment, the parent StorageLocation is authoritative; competing direct location truth is forbidden. A StockItem may be temporarily unplaced only through an explicit governed state, and every resolved placement must belong to the same Household that owns the StockItem.
7. Occupancy labels such as full/almost-full/empty are projections or observations; they must not silently become a second authoritative inventory ledger.

## Product catalog and identifiers

8. Product is the canonical stockable food/product identity and is not synonymous with a retail SKU. Every Product has explicit catalog governance: a global Product has no Household owner and cannot be mutated by ordinary Household authority merely because stock references it; a household-scoped Product belongs to exactly one Household and its visibility/edit authority is constrained to that Household. A Household may reference a global Product or its own household-scoped Product, never another Household's private Product. Promotion/sharing/merge across catalog scopes requires an explicit governed workflow with preserved provenance. Commercial identifiers, brand, manufacturer and Batch metadata remain optional, and Product identity is independent of stock, purchase price and storage location.
9. A Product may have zero, one or many identifiers. Every ProductIdentifier must carry an explicit scheme/type and the issuer/namespace required by that scheme. Identifier uniqueness and resolution are scoped by `(scheme, issuer/namespace, normalized value)`, except where the scheme itself defines a single governed global namespace. Non-global identifiers from different issuers must not collide or resolve ambiguously.
10. Price is transaction/context-specific. Product must not hold a single authoritative current unit price. Every authoritative monetary amount must carry explicit currency and exact money semantics; binary floating point is not authoritative for money. PurchaseItem monetary facts must identify their semantic role rather than use an ambiguous generic amount: a unit/basis price identifies the pricing-basis quantity and MeasurementUnit to which it applies, while line-gross, line-discount, line-tax/governed line charges and line-net amounts are explicit roles when present. The ordinary reconciliation equation is `line_net = line_gross - line_discount + line_tax/line-charges` under a governed currency scale/rounding policy. Source-provided and platform-derived values must preserve provenance and either reconcile under that policy or remain an explicit discrepancy. Purchase-level discounts/taxes/fees must not be silently folded into line unit price or historical unit cost; later derived allocations require explicit method, basis, rounding and provenance. Cross-currency comparison/conversion requires explicit rate/source and conversion time/context, and silent currency conversion is forbidden.
11. Quantities must have valid measurement semantics; every purchased, received, moved, consumed, counted, prepared-input, prepared-output, replenishment-policy, shopping or recipe quantity that participates in reconciliation/comparison must carry or resolve an explicit MeasurementUnit. Incompatible dimensions cannot be converted without an explicit product/ingredient-specific rule where required. Whenever a committed business fact depends on contextual, package-equivalence or cross-dimension conversion, it must preserve or immutably reference the exact conversion evidence used — source/target quantity and unit, factor/formula inputs, conversion profile/rule identity and version, effective/evaluation context and provenance — so later profile changes cannot reinterpret historical conservation or reconciliation. Authoritative quantities, operative conversion factors and converted results have exact rational semantics. Conservation, availability and reconciliation equality is evaluated in a declared comparison unit using lossless rational arithmetic, including non-terminating decimal results; binary floating point, implementation-specific decimal scale, display rounding or per-allocation rounding must not decide equality or erase a residual. A split remainder remains an explicit rational quantity.

## Batch and stock identity

12. Batch identity and physical stock identity are distinct. Every StockItem identifies exactly one Product directly; Batch association is optional provenance and must never be required merely to obtain Product identity.
13. Multiple StockItems may originate from the same Batch while having different placements, package states, lifecycle events or effective expiry. If a StockItem references a Batch, that Batch must belong to the same Product as the StockItem.
14. Moving a StockItem must not mutate the manufacturing/commercial identity of its Batch, and absence of known manufacturer batch information must not be represented by fabricated batch identity. A source expiration observed for a concrete StockItem/package must remain representable independently of Batch identity.

## Inventory truth

15. Business-significant stock changes must be represented by durable inventory movement semantics. Any business operation that redistributes one conserved quantity across multiple movement effects must preserve Product identity and exact total quantity after valid dimension-safe conversion unless that operation is explicitly a transformation with separately modeled inputs and outputs. When a movement changes placement, the committed history must preserve the immutable placement fact relevant to that effect. In particular, InventoryTransfer must preserve source placement on its decrement effect, destination placement on its increment effect, and authoritative domain occurrence time, so historical per-placement state can be reconstructed even after the current StockItem placement changes.
16. A materialized/current balance may exist for performance, but it must be reconcilable with authoritative stock history.
17. Stock-changing commands must define atomic concurrency behavior so two valid concurrent actions cannot silently produce an invalid balance. Inventory reconciliation must use the authoritative observation/as-of point captured for each InventoryCountItem. A session-level cutoff may substitute for per-line cutoffs only when a genuinely atomic/frozen snapshot or equivalent snapshot token guarantees all count lines correspond to the same authoritative state. A movement recorded after that cutoff must be classified by domain occurrence time: a post-observation occurrence is preserved as a genuinely intervening movement; an occurrence at or before the observation invalidates/rebases the historical reconciliation basis and must be included before the count adjustment is computed or retained. A late pre-observation movement must never be applied on top of an adjustment that already compensated for the same physical effect.
18. Retrying an idempotent stock-changing command with the same identity must not apply the quantity delta twice.
19. Committed historical movement records are immutable for business truth; corrections are represented by explicit compensating/adjustment semantics rather than rewriting committed history.
20. Physical inventory reconciliation must create an explicit adjustment or equivalent auditable outcome rather than silently overwriting history. Each InventoryCountItem must identify the counted Product, observed quantity and MeasurementUnit, plus placement when relevant to the count scope, and must preserve its authoritative physical observation time plus corresponding ledger cutoff/as-of point unless the containing session proves one common atomic snapshot under invariant 17. It may reference an existing StockItem when matched, but unmatched newly discovered physical stock must remain representable without fabricating an existing StockItem. The adjustment must be computed against the authoritative historical movement set for that line's observation time, link back to the count line, cutoff and reconciliation basis, and preserve only genuinely post-observation movements. If a newly recorded authoritative movement occurred at or before the observation, any uncommitted reconciliation is invalidated and recomputed; if an adjustment was already committed, history is preserved and a deterministic compensating/reconciliation outcome must be emitted when safe, otherwise the case must block/escalate. If multiple state-distinct StockItems are compatible with an aggregate observation and the affected holding(s) cannot be determined, the discrepancy must remain unresolved/staged or be recounted at sufficient granularity; arbitrary allocation across batch, expiration, package/lifecycle or provenance-distinct holdings is forbidden. If the historical state or deterministic allocation cannot be established safely, the system must block or escalate rather than guess.

## Purchase and receiving

21. A Purchase represents acquisition/commercial intent or transaction; Receipt represents physical entry into inventory.
22. A simple workflow may create Purchase and Receipt together, but purchased and received quantities must remain independently reconcilable at line level. Each PurchaseItem must identify its Product, quantity and MeasurementUnit and preserve explicit monetary-role semantics under invariant 10, including the pricing basis for any unit/basis price and the gross/discount/tax-or-charge/net relationship when those components exist. Each committed ReceiptItem must identify the received Product, quantity and MeasurementUnit, link to its source PurchaseItem when applicable, and retain traceable linkage to the inventory entry effect(s) that materialized the receipt. When ReceiptItem references PurchaseItem, their Products must match exactly unless an explicit governed substitution record/allocation preserves requested Product, received Product, substituted quantity/unit, reason/approval and provenance. Every linked inventory entry effect must represent the ReceiptItem Product, and the sum of committed linked entry quantities after valid dimension-safe conversion must equal exactly the ReceiptItem quantity. All ordinary same-Product receipt allocations and governed substitution allocations against one PurchaseItem consume the same physical-receiving availability pool; after exact-rational conversion their combined normal-fulfillment allocation must not exceed the PurchaseItem quantity. ShoppingListFulfillment allocations occupy a separate shopping-intent attribution pool, independently capped by PurchaseItem quantity across shopping lines. The same unit may occur once in each pool because physical receipt and intent attribution are independent facts, but no quantity may be double-counted within either pool; one pool neither changes the other's availability nor substitutes for its evidence. Any receipt excess, ordinary or substituted, is an explicit over-receipt discrepancy/exception requiring governed acceptance or correction and must not silently count as ordinary fulfillment. A legitimate ad-hoc Receipt may omit PurchaseItem provenance, but it must not omit inventory-entry provenance or quantity conservation.

## Recipes and preparation

23. A Recipe is a reusable definition and must not depend on a concrete physical Batch or StockItem. Every Recipe has exactly one explicit catalog-governance scope: global with no Household owner, or household-scoped with exactly one owning Household and visibility/edit authority constrained to it. RecipeVersion inherits that scope/owner and cannot widen it. A global Recipe may reference only globally visible catalog entities and may impose an exact-Product constraint only on a global Product; a household-scoped Recipe may reference global catalog entities or those owned by the same Household, never another Household's private Product. A Preparation may execute only a global RecipeVersion or one owned by the Preparation's Household. Cross-scope publication, sharing or cloning requires an explicit governed workflow that validates destination-scope references and preserves provenance rather than silently changing ownership or visibility. Recipe evolution is versioned: a committed recipe-based Preparation must reference an immutable RecipeVersion or equivalent immutable recipe snapshot containing the exact RecipeIngredient definitions and constraints used for that execution.
24. A RecipeIngredient describes what is required; a PreparationInput records which concrete stock fulfilled preparation consumption. Each PreparationInput must identify its source StockItem, Product, consumed quantity and MeasurementUnit and retain traceable linkage to the authoritative preparation-input InventoryMovement decrement effect(s). Those effects must represent the same Product, resolve to the referenced StockItem or governed split lineage, and sum exactly to the PreparationInput quantity after valid dimension-safe conversion. When the Preparation executes a Recipe, fulfillment of RecipeIngredient requirements must be represented through explicit measurable PreparationInputAllocation records that identify the exact immutable RecipeIngredient version/snapshot line, allocated quantity and MeasurementUnit. Each allocation must preserve or immutably reference the compatibility decision evidence used at commit time — including Product, IngredientConcept, compatibility rule/mapping identity and version, effective/evaluation time/context, and any exact-Product or other governed constraints — so later catalog compatibility edits cannot reinterpret historical validity. Repeated/similar recipe lines must remain distinguishable. For every RecipeIngredient snapshot line, the Preparation must preserve the effective required quantity after governed recipe scaling/yield adjustment together with the scaling inputs/context used; the sum of compatible allocations targeting that exact line must reconcile against that preserved effective requirement after valid conversion. Normal fulfillment may not silently underfill or overfill the requirement. Any allowed underage, overage, tolerance or substitution must be an explicit deviation preserving expected quantity, actual allocated quantity, MeasurementUnit, reason/policy and provenance/approval where required. On the source side, every recipe-based PreparationInput must also be fully accounted for: after valid conversion, the sum of its RecipeIngredient allocations plus explicitly classified non-recipe additions, process loss, waste or other governed deviations must equal exactly the committed PreparationInput quantity. An unallocated remainder is forbidden. Ad-hoc Preparations without a Recipe have no RecipeIngredient allocation requirement, but their PreparationInput quantity still must reconcile exactly with its authoritative decrement effects.
25. A Preparation is a concrete household-scoped execution. A recipe-based Preparation must remain historically reproducible from its immutable RecipeVersion/snapshot, preserved scaled requirements/constraints, compatibility-decision evidence, committed inputs/outputs, explicit source-side input accounting and authoritative movement provenance; subsequent edits to the reusable Recipe or compatibility mappings affect future executions only unless an explicit correction workflow is recorded.
26. Food produced by a Preparation must be representable as inventory output when it remains available for later storage, consumption, waste or further preparation. Each PreparationOutput must identify its Product, quantity and MeasurementUnit and retain traceable linkage to the authoritative preparation-output InventoryMovement effect(s) that materialized the output. Those effects must represent the same Product and sum exactly to the PreparationOutput quantity after valid dimension-safe conversion.
27. Preparation lineage must be preservable from output back to consumed inputs through durable provenance. Current StockItem balance, current Recipe contents, or current Product-to-IngredientConcept compatibility alone are insufficient historical lineage when inputs/outputs are split, merged, recipes are edited, compatibility rules change, or later movements occur.

## Shelf life and food lifecycle

28. Relative shelf life is a governed temporal rule/duration triggered by an authoritative fact; it is not stored as if it were an absolute calendar date. Every relative ShelfLifeRule must explicitly preserve duration amount/unit, temporal basis (`ELAPSED` or `LOCAL_CALENDAR`), endpoint semantics and any governed timezone/version context required to produce one deterministic instant. In `ELAPSED` mode, seconds/minutes/hours are exact elapsed durations, one elapsed day is exactly 24 hours and one elapsed week is exactly seven elapsed days; month/year units are invalid. In `LOCAL_CALENDAR` mode, calendar days/weeks/months/years are added in the anchored governed IANA timezone, preserving activation local wall-clock time unless the rule declares another endpoint. Month/year addition applies the full amount to the original anchored local date using proleptic-Gregorian year/month arithmetic, retaining its day-of-month when valid and otherwise clamping to the target month's final valid day; intermediate clamping must not create iterative drift. DST gaps resolve to the first valid instant after the gap; overlaps resolve to the earlier occurrence. End-of-local-day means the exclusive start of the following local day. A candidate expires at its computed boundary and is valid strictly before it. A rule missing required temporal basis/timezone/endpoint semantics is invalid and must not be applied. An authoritative source expiration may exist at StockItem/package level even when no Batch is known, and its original precision/semantics and provenance must be preserved.
29. Effective expiration of a concrete StockItem must be explainable from source expiration and applicable lifecycle/storage rules. Every ShelfLifeRule must have explicit governed applicability, semantic trigger/deadline group and version/effective-interval semantics. Precedence applies only among rules competing inside the same semantic trigger/deadline group for the same kind of deadline and authoritative activation fact: within that group, more specific scope outranks broader scope, then explicit priority resolves ordering. Independent groups or activation facts — such as stock-entry/default and opening — remain independently applicable and may each contribute a candidate; a more specific rule in one group must not suppress a deadline from another group. Version/effective-interval selection is evaluated as of the domain occurrence time of the authoritative fact that activates the relevant group: triggering lifecycle-event occurrence for event-driven rules; authoritative stock-entry occurrence for stock-entry/default rules; authoritative InventoryMovement/InventoryTransfer or other canonical placement-state occurrence for placement/storage-change rules; and trusted storage/conservation observation occurrence for observation-triggered rules. Recalculation must reuse that same original evaluation anchor rather than current/recalculation time. Calendar-based relative arithmetic must reuse the timezone version/context anchored to that activation fact unless the rule preserves a more specific governed timezone context; elapsed arithmetic is computed on the instant timeline and is not changed by later timezone/DST configuration. Equally specific conflicting rules with the same effective priority inside one competing group at that evaluation time must be rejected or surfaced for governance rather than chosen arbitrarily. Applicable source and independent rule-group deadlines form a candidate set; unless a future explicitly governed semantic-class rule defines otherwise, the effective operational expiration is the earliest applicable candidate, so no later candidate may extend an earlier authoritative deadline. A source expiration expressed only as a calendar date preserves date-only precision as source truth but, for operational ordering, is interpreted as the end of that local calendar day in the canonical Household timezone applicable to the StockItem, unless the source itself supplies an explicit timezone/offset or governed source context. When Household timezone is used, the exact timezone version must be selected as of the preserved domain occurrence time at which that SourceExpirationFact became authoritative for the concrete StockItem/package — normally the fact's source observation/import/receipt occurrence time — unless a more specific governed source temporal context is preserved. Initial calculation and every recomputation must reuse this same timezone-selection anchor; later Household timezone changes cannot reinterpret the historical date-only fact. Instant candidates retain exact instant semantics; operational normalization must not mutate the original source precision. Any storage/conservation fact allowed to influence shelf-life must come from canonical placement/state or a trusted observation with occurrence time and provenance; uncertain sensor/heuristic evidence must not silently rewrite authoritative shelf-life inputs. EffectiveExpiration must retain provenance to the candidate set, semantic groups, group-local precedence decisions, source expiration fact(s), rule evaluation anchor, relative-duration temporal basis/unit/endpoint/timezone context, selected ShelfLifeRule version(s), storage/conservation inputs, the date-only SourceExpirationFact occurrence/source anchor, selected timezone version/context and combination result.
30. Opening, freezing, thawing, preparing or other lifecycle changes may alter shelf-life semantics without changing Product identity.
31. Expiration does not prove physical disposal.
32. A scheduled job may detect/flag expiration and create alerts, but it must not assert that food was physically discarded without an explicit domain action or trusted external evidence.

## Audit, provenance and time

33. Domain occurrence time and system recording/commit time are distinct where delayed/offline recording is possible, and changing recording time must never rewrite domain occurrence semantics. Physical inventory counts and any other delayed reconciliation workflow must preserve the domain occurrence/as-of point required to evaluate historical state correctly; for non-atomic counting sessions this requirement applies at InventoryCountItem granularity. Late-arriving facts that occurred before an observation must rebase/invalidate dependent historical projections or reconciliations rather than being treated as later physical actions.
34. Important external or automated observations/mutations must retain source/provenance such as user action, barcode scan, vision result, import, automation or system process. Heuristic or untrusted identification output — including vision/scanner candidates — is evidence/proposal, not canonical Product, StockItem or inventory truth by itself; canonicalization requires governed matching/review/reconciliation appropriate to confidence and ambiguity. Any committed decision whose validity depends on mutable governed reference data — including Product-to-IngredientConcept compatibility — must preserve the exact version/effective context/evidence used at that decision point rather than relying on the current mapping later.
35. AuditEvent is not a replacement for inventory movement history, domain events, application logs or security telemetry.
36. Auditable actions must be attributable to an actor or trusted system principal and to the relevant Household/context when applicable. Household-derived AlertRule, Alert and NotificationDelivery records must preserve an explicit same-Household ownership chain: the rule records its governed subject/scope; the Alert retains its originating rule, triggering subject/context and Household; and each NotificationDelivery retains its Alert plus intended recipient/destination, channel and delivery provenance. A genuinely user-global preference may influence delivery behavior but must not itself grant access to Household operational data, create a cross-household alert rule or permit a delivery to be reassigned across Household boundaries. Recipient address, device token, webhook destination or provider identity is not proof of Household authorization.

## Configuration and integrations

37. Canonical relational household/storage structure must not be duplicated as an authoritative JSON configuration document.
38. Integration credentials and secrets must not be stored as arbitrary domain JSON or returned to clients.
39. External integrations/imports must pass through normalization/matching/reconciliation semantics before becoming canonical inventory state when duplication or ambiguity is possible. Any Integration authorized to read or affect Household-scoped operational data must resolve through an explicit governed Household scope/binding; provider identity, provider account ownership or possession of credentials is not Household authorization. Every inventory-affecting ImportRun must identify exactly one target Household, and every ExternalReference used to reconcile/materialize Household inventory must retain that same Household scope directly or through an unambiguous parent. Resulting canonical entities/effects must remain inside that target Household boundary. Imported identifiers and monetary values must preserve their source namespace/issuer and currency/conversion provenance rather than being normalized into ambiguous values.

## Deletion and lifecycle

40. Deletion/archival rules must preserve required financial, inventory, lineage and audit history. Referential history must not be destroyed merely to make an entity disappear from normal UI views.

## System-wide rule

41. No implementation convenience — ORM behavior, frontend state, trigger, scheduled job, cache, external integration or deployment topology — may override these invariants. If an implementation cannot preserve an invariant, the implementation must change or the invariant must be explicitly revisited through governance.

## Planning and replenishment derived corollary

This section is a derived restatement of the numbered invariants for planning/replenishment clarity; it adds no independent invariant beyond the 41 rules above.

A measurable HouseholdProductPolicy threshold, including minimum desired stock, must carry or resolve a MeasurementUnit and may be compared with inventory only through accepted dimension-safe conversion semantics. A resolved ShoppingListItem must target exactly one canonical subject — Product or IngredientConcept — and carry requested quantity and MeasurementUnit. Free text may be retained as unresolved input/provenance but must not silently serve as canonical fulfillment identity.

Fulfillment must be represented as explicit quantity allocation from PurchaseItem to ShoppingListItem. Product-targeted intent accepts only the exact Product; IngredientConcept-targeted intent accepts only Products satisfying the governed compatibility relationship effective at the fulfillment decision point. That compatibility decision must preserve or immutably reference its mapping/rule identity and version, effective/evaluation context and provenance so later compatibility changes cannot reinterpret committed fulfillment. Allocated quantities must reconcile under accepted exact-rational unit conversion, must not exceed the source PurchaseItem quantity available in the distinct shopping-intent pool after its other ShoppingListFulfillment allocations, and must not be double-counted across shopping lines. Physical receipt/substitution allocations belong to an independent pool and do not consume shopping-intent availability. Partial, full, over-fulfillment, substitution and tolerance semantics must be explicit; a line must not be marked fully fulfilled merely because a related PurchaseItem exists.
